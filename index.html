At clvl 35 you've earned roughly 38 points (34 from leveling + 4 from Normal's Den of Evil, Radament, and Izual). That covers the 28-point core with ~10 left for Fire Ball — the * above. Respec any earlier than 35 and that Fire Ball remainder shrinks fast: at clvl 30 you're carrying only ~33 points, leaving just 5 for Fire Ball. Nothing here goes negative either way, but 33–35 gives Fire Ball a healthier chunk to reopen with.

Open question: Ice Bolt vs. Fire Mastery order

resolved
Which should you max first after Fire Ball, at level 35+?
Take Ice Bolt before Fire Mastery.
  • The surplus removes the tradeoff. With 9–14 spare points by clvl 99, this isn't "can I afford both" — it's purely about which lands sooner. You max both either way.
  • Ice Bolt buffs your primary skill. It's Frozen Orb's synergy, and Frozen Orb is mapped to your opener (X). Fire Mastery only helps Fire Ball, which the note already frames as the cold-immune / single-target backup on Y.
  • Earlier Ice Bolt points compound sooner. Every point invested while leveling from 35→70ish is buffing the skill you're actually opening every pack with, versus a flat multiplier sitting on your secondary skill until you get around to it.
  • Nothing is lost by going Ice Bolt first. Since both hit 20/20 by end-game under either order, there's no final-build cost — only a smoother mid-level clear speed curve.

where your gear lands

Stealth (25% FCR) plus one Spirit sword (20–35% FCR, roll-dependent) already totals 45–60% FCR — comfortably past the 37% breakpoint (20 frames), a real jump from the 23-frame base. It falls short of 63% (19 frames) on its own. The note's own plan for a second Spirit in the shield slot (Hell) pushes total FCR to roughly 65–95%, clearing 63% reliably. The top breakpoint, 105% (18 frames), needs dedicated FCR gear beyond what's in the runeword list — treat it as a later, optional target rather than something to plan around now.

gap

Nothing in the current runeword plan touches FHR. Stealth and Spirit both skip it entirely, so without a dedicated boots slot you're stuck at the 15-frame base. A boot with 20–30% FHR (Infernostride, Silkweave, or a rare/magic pair) clears the 20% breakpoint (11 frames) and is worth adding to the gear list — getting chain-stunned while your cooldowns are up is the actual cause of most caster deaths, not raw damage taken.

Resistances & the Hell penalty

per resist type

Each difficulty applies a flat penalty to all four resistances (fire, cold, lightning, poison), subtracted from whatever your gear and quest rewards actually total. The 75% cap doesn't move — what moves is how much raw resistance you need to reach it.

DifficultyPenaltyResist capRaw resist needed
Normal0%75%75%
Nightmare−40%75%115%
Hell−100%75%175%
built-in help

Malah's Prison of Ice quest (Act 5) hands you a scroll worth +10% to all resistances — permanent, once per difficulty. Clear it in Normal, Nightmare, and Hell and you're carrying +30% baked in before gear even enters the picture, cutting the Hell requirement from 175% down to roughly 145% from skills and items.

gap

The Sorceress has no defensive resist skill of her own — Cold Mastery and Fire Mastery lower enemy resistance, they don't raise yours. Of the four runewords on the list, only Lore contributes (a flat Lightning Resist bonus); Leaf, Stealth, and Spirit give none. Resist-all needs to be tracked as its own gear category — rings, amulet, and charms are the usual places to close the gap, and this is worth pricing out before you hit Hell rather than after.

Mercenary plan

not in the original note

Sorceresses have no minion or defensive skill of their own, so the merc isn't a convenience — it's the build's only source of physical damage and melee aggro. Hire from Act 2 Nightmare, not Normal: a merc's aura level and stats lock in at whatever difficulty you hired them from, and a Nightmare hire scales through Hell fine, while a Normal one doesn't.

An item's granted aura (from a runeword like Infinity, below) runs alongside the merc's own class aura, not instead of it — so the Offensive merc's Might (physical damage boost) stacks with whatever aura his weapon grants. That's the whole reason to hire Offensive over Holy Freeze or Prayer.

Merc survivability isn't covered anywhere else in this plan. A geared merc tanks hits your Sorceress can't afford to take; a naked one dies on repeat in Hell and stops contributing Might or Conviction when you need it most. Budget for a helm (Vampire Gaze is the classic cheap-ish pick) and a defensive armor (Duriel's Shell or a Treachery runeword) alongside the weapon progression — treat this as its own line item, not something that falls out of your own gear farming.
