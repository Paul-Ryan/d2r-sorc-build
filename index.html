Diablo II: Resurrected · Fire Ball → Frozen Orb

Paul's Sorceress Build Guide

Every skill point from level 2 to 99, the gear that carries it there, and the numbers the build actually has to hit.

110 skill points total by level 99 — 98 from leveling, 12 from quest rewards (Den of Evil, Radament's Lair, The Fallen Angel/Izual — once per difficulty each, all low-risk quests almost every character finishes anyway). Assumed throughout below.

The leveling build — Normal, to level 35

does this add up?

This build spends 29 points by level 29. Levels 2–29 hand out 28, and Den of Evil covers the difference — it is the point you're holding at 11. Everything beyond that is slack: Radament's Lair and Izual land whenever they land, and each simply moves you down the rail a level sooner. Nothing to recompute, and no schedule to fall behind — the order is the plan, and the gates are the only levels that constrain it. The rail keeps running past 29 to the respec at 35 — those last few levels are what Phase 2's 38-point count is built on.

why this order

Rows 1–6 are forced: Frozen Orb can't be bought without its five prerequisites, and it's the skill the whole respec exists for. After that the order is a judgement. Fire Ball finishes first (row 7) because you're mid-fight with it the moment you stand up from Akara. Fire Mastery's single point comes next because it's the largest return on one point anywhere in this build. Ice Bolt before Fire Bolt because Frozen Orb is your opener, mapped to X, and points there compound over every pack you clear from 40 to 70 — whereas Fire Ball is the answer to cold-immunes specifically, so Fire Bolt needs to be well underway by Hell rather than before it. Cold Mastery lands between them for the same reason: it amplifies the skill you're actually opening with. Fire Mastery's remainder goes dead last on purpose — at roughly +4% a point those are the weakest points in the plan, and nothing is gated behind them.

why 35 and not 30

At level 35 you've earned 38 points (34 from leveling + 4 from Normal's Den of Evil, Radament, and Izual) — which is exactly where the line above falls, leaving 7 for Fire Ball. Respec at 30 instead and you're carrying only 33: the first five rows still fit, but Fire Ball gets 2. Nothing goes negative either way. The five levels are what keep Fire Ball usable while you rebuild everything else.

how many respecs you actually get

Akara resets stats and skills together — there's no way to reset one without the other — free once per difficulty, so three for the character's life. This spends the first. Holding a second for when a Monarch turns up (see Stats & Keys) still leaves one spare, and beyond that a Token of Absolution — cube the four Essences from Hell's act bosses — is unlimited. You are not short on respecs. The coupling is why the list above is written as an order rather than a destination: the Monarch reset re-enters it from the top.

Fire Mastery is the obvious multiplier for the fire side. Why does row 13 stop at 11 instead of 20?
Because Fire Bolt buys about twice as much per point — and your gear only ever helps Fire Mastery.
  • Fire Mastery is a separate multiplier, not a synergy. Damage is base × (1 + synergies) × (1 + mastery). Because the two brackets multiply, each one's value depends only on how full it already is — stacking Fire Bolt never devalues a mastery point, and the reverse holds too.
  • Its curve is brutally front-loaded. The first point is worth +30%. Every point after it adds +7%, and because that +7 is measured against an ever-larger multiplier, the real marginal value falls to about +4% almost immediately. That's why row 8 exists separately from row 13.
  • +skills raise Fire Mastery and do nothing for Fire Bolt. Synergies count hard points only — gear, charms and shrines never touch them. Your +5 to +7 from Lore and the Spirits inflates Fire Mastery's level for free, making its first point enormous and every later point worse, since they climb a curve the gear already pushed up.
  • So Fire Bolt wins the middle of the budget. A Fire Bolt point is worth +14% of base damage and is untouched by all of the above. It out-earns a second Fire Mastery point until Fire Bolt is around 18.
  • The difference is not small. For the same points, this order lands near ×8.1 on Fire Ball's damage; taking Fire Mastery to 20 and leaving Fire Bolt at 1 lands near ×6.0. Roughly 35% more damage for an identical spend.
rows 11 and 13 are the swing — and a charm decides them

Cold Mastery 10 and Fire Mastery 11 is the default, but five points slide between them and the right answer depends on one item. Cold Mastery is linear — −20% enemy cold resist at level 1, −5% per level after — with no diminishing returns until it hits the floor. Pushing it 10→15 is worth about +15% Frozen Orb damage against a typical resistant monster; the five points it costs are worth about +17% on Fire Ball instead. Genuinely close. The tiebreak: without a Cold Rupture sunder charm, cold-immune packs are Fire Ball's problem alone, so the points belong in Fire Mastery. With one, Frozen Orb handles immunes too and those same Cold Mastery points pay +21% there — so swap to Cold Mastery 15 / Fire Mastery 6 the day a Cold Rupture drops. You'll be respeccing for the Monarch anyway.

the −100% floor

A monster's cold resistance cannot be driven below −100%, and with your +5 to +7 skills a hard Cold Mastery of about 11 already reaches it. Past that point, extra Cold Mastery does nothing against a monster that started at 0% cold resist — it only helps against ones carrying positive cold resistance to begin with. That's the real reason row 11 stops at 10 rather than being pushed on principle: the headline number keeps climbing long after the damage stops.

Damage is 25% of a monster's current life at every skill level — points buy radius and nothing else, at about 0.67 yards each. Meanwhile the plan already carries +5 to skills from Lore and two Spirits, and a +2 Sorceress amulet makes it +7, which runs a single-point Static Field at around 8 yards for free. That's the whole case: one point, and the gear you were buying anyway does the rest. In Hell it takes roughly three casts and 27 mana to walk a boss down to the 50% floor, and then Frozen Orb or Fire Ball finishes the job.

caveat — Infinity does not fix this one

Static Field is lightning damage, scaled by the target's lightning resistance, so it does less than 25% against resistant monsters and nothing at all against lightning immunes. The part that catches people: Conviction from Infinity does not help — resistance debuffs are ignored for Static Field's calculation, so the item carrying your whole Hell plan can't open this door. Treat Static as a tool for bosses and tough uniques that happen not to be lightning immune, not as a universal opener.

why this order

Stealth and Leaf come first because they're the only two you can finish entirely in Normal — every rune they need clears Normal Countess. Ancients' Pledge follows immediately behind them, because two of its three runes are Normal drops as well and the odd one out is an Ort — which arrives with Lore's anyway. what "Hell farming" actually means for Infinity

Hell Countess tops out at Ist, so her bonus drop still covers Infinity's Mal and one Ist — keep running her. The two Ber runes are the real bottleneck: Countess can't drop them at any difficulty, and there's no single boss that reliably does either. The realistic spots are Lower Kurast super chests (fast per attempt, no fighting required), Travincal (Council Members have a shot at high runes), and the Secret Cow Level (several hundred monsters per run, so volume compensates for low odds) — plus whatever Terror Zones you're running once you're geared enough for those. Expect this to take a while; a lot of players trade for Ber runes rather than farm them out directly.

Larzuk, vendors, the Cube — reference

Runes are the easy half. Every runeword also needs a base with exactly the right number of sockets. The first three below you can buy outright from vendors; the 4-socket bases are the real bottleneck, and this is general mechanics you'll want to know before you hit it, not something tied to one difficulty.

three charges, four wants

Larzuk sockets one item per difficulty — three for the character's entire life — and this plan has four items that need a 4-socket base: the Spirit sword, the Monarch, and two merc polearms. Something has to come from the Cube. Spend Larzuk on the two you'll carry longest — the Spirit sword in Normal and the Monarch in Hell — and cube the polearms, since the merc's weapon gets replaced anyway when Insight becomes Infinity.

either sword works — match the method to the base

Broad Sword and Crystal Sword both hold Spirit and roll identical stats, so take whichever drops first. The Crystal Sword is even slightly cheaper — 43 strength instead of 48 — at the cost of half the durability. What does differ is how you socket it, because Larzuk gives the maximum a base can hold. A Broad Sword caps at 4 no matter what, so it is always safe to hand him. A Crystal Sword above ilvl 40 maxes at six, and a 6-socket sword cannot hold Spirit — the reward is wasted and the quest doesn't come back. So: Broad Sword → Larzuk, Crystal Sword → Cube, and only give Larzuk a Crystal Sword if you know it's ilvl 26–40.

goal: Leaf & Stealth

Runewords only, and nothing else. The resist penalty is 0% here, so spend nothing chasing it — every rune the Countess drops and every spare gold piece goes toward these two.

Everything else, including Charsi's Imbue, is safe to walk past.

shopping the base

A Kite Shield caps at 3 sockets, which is exactly what the runeword wants — so unlike the Crystal Sword there's no way to overshoot and ruin it. Buy it already socketed from Fara in Act 2 Normal, resetting her stock at the waypoint until a white or gray 3-socket one appears; a Large Shield is the fallback if Kite Shields won't roll. What you must not do is spend a Larzuk charge here — all three are already committed to the Spirit sword and the Monarch. And ignore the shield's block number entirely: block chance is (blocking × (dex − 15)) / (level × 2), which at your base 25 dexterity is roughly 3%. Never pay strength for a better-blocking base you can't use.

the Monarch swap is a resist downgrade

Worth knowing before you get attached: when the Hell Spirit shield finally replaces this, your resists go down. Spirit's shield half carries cold, lightning, and poison at 35% each and no fire at all — so the swap costs you 48% fire, 13 lightning, 13 poison, and 8 cold, in exchange for +2 skills, cast rate, hit recovery, vitality, and mana. That trade is still right, because the skills and FCR are worth more than any shield's resists by then. But it's the moment the fire gap gets worse, not better, and your rings, amulet, and charms have to be ready to absorb it.

open slots start mattering now

Once the three runewords above are moving, the six open slots — amulet, two rings, gloves, belt, boots — are where every remaining number comes from: the FCR you're short of, and all of your resistance. They're carrying two jobs competing for the same affixes, and resist all wins — an uncapped resist in Hell kills you, a missed cast breakpoint just makes you slower. Take Malah's Prison of Ice scroll the moment it's available, and hire your Act 2 Offensive merc from Nightmare, not Normal — his aura level locks in at the difficulty you hire him from.

Infinity does not actually solve immunity

The plan leans on Conviction to open cold- and fire-immune packs, and it only half does. Resistance reduction from any source — Conviction, Lower Resist, Cold Mastery — applies at one fifth strength against a monster that is already immune (100% or more). So level 12 Conviction's −85% lands as roughly −17%, which breaks immunities up to about 116% and nothing above it. A lot of Hell's cold immunes sit higher than that and stay immune with Infinity equipped. Cold Mastery can't help either — it is explicitly unable to break an immunity, at any level. The actual answer is a Sunder Charm (see Beyond Hell): Cold Rupture forces an immune monster to 95%, after which Cold Mastery starts working on it, still at one fifth. Until you hold one, treat cold-immune packs as Fire Ball's job and nothing else's.

fire resistance is the real gap

Running Lore + Stealth + both Spirits and all three Scrolls of Resistance lands you around fire 30%, cold 65%, lightning 95%, poison 95% raw — against the 175% Hell needs for a capped 75%. Every element is short, but fire is the hole: rings, amulet, and charms have to close roughly a 145% fire gap. Charms are where the last stretch of resist comes from once your six equipment slots are picked. Only once resists are capped should you spend anything chasing the 105% FCR breakpoint — see Breakpoints & Resists for both tables in full.

the Monarch decision, deferred

The Hell off-hand Spirit goes in a Monarch: 156 strength, and that is not a rounding error — 146 points over your base 10, roughly 29% of the lifetime stat budget, permanent from the moment you equip it. Don't pre-pay it. Carry only what your sword asks for (43–48), put everything else in Vitality, and respec into the Monarch once you actually hold one — you get a free reset per difficulty and the plan only spends one. Charms and +all-stat gear (Annihilus, Hellfire Torch) claw back 20–40 of the cost. And Monarch is the floor here, not a preference: it's the lowest-strength 4-socket non-class shield in the game, so there's no cheaper base to find.

Mercenary plan

not in the original note

Sorceresses have no minion or defensive skill of their own, so the merc isn't a convenience — it's the build's only source of physical damage and melee aggro. Hire from Act 2 Nightmare, not Normal: a merc's aura level and stats lock in at whatever difficulty you hired them from, and a Nightmare hire scales through Hell fine, while a Normal one doesn't.

pick offensive / might

An item's granted aura (from a runeword like Infinity, below) runs alongside the merc's own class aura, not instead of it — so the Offensive merc's Might (physical damage boost) stacks with whatever aura his weapon grants. That's the whole reason to hire Offensive over Holy Freeze or Prayer.

Merc survivability isn't covered anywhere else in this plan. A geared merc tanks hits your Sorceress can't afford to take; a naked one dies on repeat in Hell and stops contributing Might or Conviction when you need it most. Budget for a helm (Vampire Gaze is the classic cheap-ish pick) and a defensive armor (Duriel's Shell or a Treachery runeword) alongside the weapon progression — treat this as its own line item, not something that falls out of your own gear farming.

where your gear lands

Stealth (25%) plus one Spirit sword (25–35%, roll-dependent) totals 50–60% FCR — comfortably past the 37% breakpoint (20 frames), a real jump from the 23-frame base, but short of 63% (19 frames) on its own. A second Spirit in the shield slot (Hell) brings the total to 75–95%, clearing 63% reliably. The top breakpoint, 105% (18 frames), needs dedicated FCR gear beyond the runeword list — 10% from each ring, 10–20% from the amulet, 20% from gloves — so treat it as a later target rather than something to plan around now.

FHR is already solved

Stealth carries 25% FHR and Spirit carries 55% — together 80%, which clears the 60% breakpoint and drops you to 8 frames from a 15-frame base. Add the second Spirit in Hell and you're at 135%, past the final 86% breakpoint at 7 frames, the fastest the Sorceress can recover. Don't spend a gear slot chasing FHR — the runeword plan maxes it for free. Note this cuts the other way too: drop Spirit for a non-runeword weapon and you lose 55% FHR along with the skills and cast rate.

Resistances & the Hell penalty

per resist type

Each difficulty applies a flat penalty to all four resistances (fire, cold, lightning, poison), subtracted from whatever your gear and quest rewards actually total. The 75% cap doesn't move — what moves is how much raw resistance you need to reach it.

DifficultyPenaltyResist capRaw resist needed
Normal0%75%75%
Nightmare−40%75%115%
Hell−100%75%175%
built-in help

Malah's Prison of Ice quest (Act 5) hands you a scroll worth +10% to all resistances — permanent, once per difficulty. Clear it in Normal, Nightmare, and Hell and you're carrying +30% baked in before gear even enters the picture, cutting the Hell requirement from 175% down to roughly 145% from skills and items.

gap — fire resistance specifically

The Sorceress has no defensive resist skill of her own — Cold Mastery and Fire Mastery lower enemy resistance, they don't raise yours. The runewords do contribute, but unevenly. Running Lore + Stealth + both Spirits in Hell, and counting three Scrolls of Resistance, you land at roughly fire 30%, cold 65%, lightning 95%, poison 95% raw — against the 175% Hell needs for a capped 75%. Every element is short, but fire is the hole: not one item in the plan grants a point of it. Rings, amulet, and charms have to close a ~145% fire gap and ~110% cold gap, and that's worth pricing out before Hell rather than after.
